## How to Use This Snapshot

This is a **consulting diagnostic tool**, not a league table. Use it to:
1. Quickly identify where a client's metrics fall relative to industry range
2. Open a conversation with: *"We're seeing the industry median sitting at X — where are you tracking?"*
3. Identify which gaps represent the highest-leverage consulting opportunities

**Benchmark tiers used throughout:**
- **Best-in-class:** Top quartile of FMCG global leaders (P&G, Unilever, Nestlé benchmark)
- **Industry median:** Mid-range across Tier 1 universe
- **Laggard:** Bottom quartile — often where the largest transformation opportunities sit

## SECTION 1: FINANCIAL METRICS

| KPI | Definition | Best-in-Class | Industry Median | Laggard | India Context | SHEI Annotation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue Growth (%)** | YoY net revenue growth | 8–12% | 4–7% | <2% | India median 5–8%; rural drag pulling down in FY24–25 | Signal: Growth deceleration despite premiumization → Hypothesis: Volume-led growth era structurally over for mass FMCG |
| **Gross Margin (%)** | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | 55–65% | 42–52% | <35% | HUL ~50%; ITC consumer ~58%; Marico ~47% | Signal: Margin recovery stalling → Hypothesis: Input cost tailwinds exhausted; mix shift needed |
| **EBITDA Margin (%)** | EBITDA / Revenue | 22–28% | 15–20% | <10% | HUL ~23%; Dabur ~18%; Britannia ~16% | Input cost volatility and A&P reinvestment are the key drivers of variance |
| **Working Capital (Days)** | Net working capital / (Revenue/365) | Negative to 0 | 15–30 days | >45 days | HUL runs negative WC; most India players 20–35 days | Best-in-class = distributors finance inventory; transformation opportunity for mid-tier players |
| **NPD Sales (%)** | Revenue from products launched in last 3 years / Total revenue | 25–35% | 12–20% | <8% | India NPD success rate lower due to GT distribution constraints | Low NPD % = innovation engine broken or GTM can't support launches |
| **Trade Spend (% of Revenue)** | Total trade promotions / Revenue | 8–12% | 14–18% | >22% | India mid-tier players often 18–22%; inefficient promo structures | High trade spend with low volume response = broken trade promotion optimization |
| **Digital Sales Penetration (%)** | Online channel revenue / Total revenue | 20–30% (global) | 8–15% | <5% | India: HUL ~15-16%; most India players 5–12% | Quick-commerce disrupting traditional e-comm metrics; need to track separately |
| **A&P as % of Revenue** | Advertising & promotion spend / Revenue | 10–14% | 7–10% | <5% | India large players 8–12%; small players often underspending | Very low A&P = brand equity erosion risk; very high = volume not responding to spend |

## SECTION 2: SUPPLY CHAIN METRICS

| KPI | Definition | Best-in-Class | Industry Median | Laggard | India Context | SHEI Annotation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Forecast Accuracy (%)** | (1 - MAPE) at 4-week horizon | 80–88% | 65–75% | <55% | India estimated 55–70% for most players; quick-commerce making it worse | Signal: Promo-driven demand spikes → Hypothesis: Traditional time-series forecasting architecturally broken for modern FMCG |
| **Perfect Order Rate (%)** | Orders delivered complete, on-time, undamaged, correctly invoiced | 92–97% | 82–90% | <75% | India: [DATA GAP — benchmark needed from primary source] | Best proxy for overall SC reliability; single metric CXOs understand immediately |
| **OTIF (%)** | On-Time In-Full delivery to retail/distributor | 90–96% | 80–88% | <72% | India general trade OTIF often 70–80% due to distributor variability | Low OTIF = revenue leakage + lost shelf space; most clients undertrack this |
| **Inventory Days** | Average inventory / (COGS/365) | 28–40 days | 45–65 days | >90 days | India: HUL ~45 days; regional players 60–90 days | High inventory days + aging inventory = cash flow pressure + obsolescence cost |
| **Inventory Turnover Ratio** | COGS / Average inventory | 9–13x | 5–8x | <4x | Inverse of inventory days; same story | |
| **Aging Inventory (%)** | Inventory >90 days old / Total inventory | <3% | 5–10% | >15% | India: Significant issue in Foods/Beverages with short shelf life | Aging inventory = demand forecast failure made visible |
| **On-Shelf Availability (%)** | % of SKUs available on shelf when audited | 92–97% | 84–91% | <78% | India general trade OSA often 75–85% due to last-mile gaps | OSA is where SC performance becomes revenue performance |
| **Return Rate (%)** | Returns / Total orders shipped | <1% | 1.5–3% | >5% | India: Varies significantly by channel; MT lower than GT | High returns in GT = expiry, damage, or over-stocking driven by push model |
| **Supplier OTIF (%)** | % of supplier deliveries on time and in full | 88–95% | 78–87% | <70% | India: Highly variable; FMCG companies often manage this poorly | Supplier OTIF directly translates to production reliability |
| **SC Cost as % of Revenue** | Total supply chain cost / Revenue | 6–9% | 10–14% | >18% | India: Distribution cost alone often 8–12% | SC cost is the lever most mid-tier Indian FMCG companies have not pulled |

## SECTION 3: SALES & DISTRIBUTION METRICS

| KPI | Definition | Best-in-Class | Industry Median | Laggard | India Context | SHEI Annotation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Numeric Distribution (%)** | % of relevant outlets stocking the product | 85–95% | 65–80% | <50% | India: HUL flagship ~90%; regional brands often 40–60% | Numeric dist = reach; weighted dist = revenue-relevant reach |
| **Weighted Distribution (%)** | Distribution weighted by outlet revenue potential | 88–96% | 70–85% | <60% | India: More meaningful than numeric in GT context | Weighted dist gap = you're in small shops but not in volume shops |
| **Effective Coverage (%)** | Productive outlets / Total outlets covered | 75–85% | 55–70% | <45% | India: Significant coverage inefficiency in many players | Low effective coverage = sales force cost not translating to revenue |
| **Avg. Sales per Outlet (₹)** | Monthly revenue / Active outlets | ₹2,500–5,000 | ₹800–2,000 | <₹400 | India benchmark varies hugely by category and geography | Low avg. sales per outlet + high coverage = wrong channel strategy |
| **Avg. Lines per Outlet** | Average SKUs selling per outlet visit | 5–8 | 3–5 | <2 | India: 3–4 lines typical for mid-tier; HUL ~5–7 | Low lines per outlet = distribution width without depth |
| **Salesperson Throughput** | Revenue generated per salesperson per month | ₹18–30L | ₹8–15L | <₹5L | India benchmark | Throughput is the key efficiency metric for GT-heavy models |
| **Direct Distribution Growth (%)** | YoY growth in direct-to-retailer coverage | 15–25% | 5–12% | Declining | India: Active push by HUL, ITC, Marico to increase direct reach | DTR growth = reducing stockist dependency |
| **E-commerce Channel Growth (%)** | YoY revenue growth from online channels | 25–45% | 15–25% | <10% | India: Quick-commerce growing 40–60%; general e-comm 15–25% | Q-commerce now the real signal; track separately from Amazon/Flipkart |
